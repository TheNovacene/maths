## Decision — always use precise mathematical vocabulary wherever possible

NEO Mathematics is a language as much as a subject. Wherever a correct mathematical term
exists, **it is used in preference to an informal paraphrase**, in every learner-facing
surface — lesson prose, interactive readouts, feedback messages, practice questions and
worked solutions — and in the Learner and Supporting-Adult guidance. The informal phrasing
may appear **once, as a brief gloss in parentheses**, at the point where a term is first
introduced; thereafter the correct term stands alone.

This formalises a standard already applied in earlier decisions (e.g. "scale factor" not
"size"; "similar" in its exact sense; "vertical/horizontal" not "up/along"; "opposite,
adjacent, hypotenuse" named relative to a marked angle). It is now a general rule, not a
per-case correction.

### Examples of the standard

| Use this (correct) | Not this (informal) |
| --- | --- |
| numerator / denominator | top / bottom, on top / underneath |
| the denominator (bottom of the fraction) — as a one-time gloss only | "the number underneath" as the standing term |
| scale factor | size |
| similar (equal angles, sides in the same ratio) | "the same shape", "roughly alike" |
| opposite / adjacent / hypotenuse (relative to θ) | the tall one / the bottom one |
| vertical / horizontal | up / along |

The prompting case that triggered this entry: Lesson TR-06 (Finding a side) originally said
the unknown was "underneath" the ratio; it now says the unknown is in the **denominator**,
with "(the bottom of the fraction)" as a single first-use gloss.

### Conditions

1. **Correct term first, gloss once.** On first use, a term may carry a short plain-language
   gloss in parentheses; after that, use the term unadorned.
2. **Age-appropriate, not dumbed-down.** These are iGCSE-aligned learners; precise vocabulary
   is part of the entitlement, introduced with care rather than avoided.
3. **Consistency across surfaces.** The same term is used in the lesson, its Practice
   Companion, its feedback and its guidance PDFs — no drift between them.
4. **Accepted-answer tolerance stays generous.** Precise *display* vocabulary does not narrow
   *marking*: answer checking still accepts correct informal synonyms a learner might type
   (e.g. "bottom" as well as "denominator").

## Rationale

Learning to name things exactly is not pedantry — it is how mathematicians avoid talking past
one another, and it is a large part of what it means to understand a concept rather than to
have a vague picture of it. Informal paraphrases feel friendlier but quietly deny learners the
words they need to reason precisely and to read the wider subject. A single first-use gloss
keeps the door open without letting the imprecise term become the standing name.

## Implications and QA

- **QA (vocabulary sweep):** before a lesson ships, scan learner-facing text and guidance for
  informal position/quantity terms (top, bottom, on top, underneath, size, "the same shape",
  up/along) and replace with the correct term, keeping at most one first-use gloss.
- **Retrofit, low priority:** earlier lessons that use informal terms (e.g. TR-05 uses "on
  top") may be aligned to "numerator" when convenient, especially where paired lessons
  cross-reference each other; not urgent, but tracked.
