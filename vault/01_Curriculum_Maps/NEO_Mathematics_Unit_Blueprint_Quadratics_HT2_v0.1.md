# NEO Mathematics — Unit Blueprint: Quadratics (Sequences, Structure and the Parabola)
**Pathway:** Foundations (internal stage code Y9) · **Term:** Autumn, Half-Term 2 — opening unit
**Shape:** 7 lessons · 45 minutes each · opens HT2 and fills roughly the first half of the 14-lesson Year 9 half-term; a second Foundations unit follows.
**Qualification overlay:** NC KS3–KS4 Algebra (quadratic sequences; expanding and factorising; graphs of quadratic functions) building towards Pearson Edexcel International GCSE (4MA1) Algebra. **Out of scope for this introductory unit:** completing the square / vertex form, the quadratic formula, solving quadratic equations algebraically, and factorising where a ≠ 1. Factorising simple quadratics (x² + bx + c) appears only as a **stretch tier**.
**Strand:** Algebra — the **first Algebra-and-graphs unit** in the Foundations pathway (follows Accuracy, Powers and Scale; draws on Pythagoras square-number work and KS3 coordinates).
**Status (v0.1):** blueprint framed; lessons to be built and reviewed one at a time.

## Unit Driving Question
When a pattern grows faster and faster, what stays steady underneath it — and how does that same hidden rule show up as a symbol you can write, and as a curve you can draw?

## The mathematical story
A quadratic is what change looks like when **the change itself is changing at a steady rate**. This unit introduces that idea by meeting a quadratic through its **three faces** and showing they are the same object seen three ways.

It begins with the **number face**: a sequence whose terms grow, where the *first* differences are not constant — but the *second* differences are. That constant second difference is the fingerprint of a quadratic, and noticing "what stays constant amid change" is the unit's central habit of mind. From there the learner meets the **symbol face** — the x² expression sitting behind the pattern — and builds and takes apart simple quadratic expressions using an **area model** (expanding for everyone; factorising as a stretch for ready learners). Then comes the **shape face**: plotting y = x² and discovering why it bends into a symmetric U, then using a live graphing environment to see how the numbers in the equation **move and reshape** that curve — the coefficient a stretching and reflecting it (y = ax²), and the number p sliding it sideways and carrying its turning point with it (y = (x − p)²). The unit closes by reading **real parabolas** in context — the arc of a thrown ball, a bridge cable, a fountain — where the turning point becomes a genuine maximum or minimum with a meaning.

**Arc:** notice the constant second difference → the x² behind it → meet the parabola → stretch & reflect it (a) → slide it (p): turning point & symmetry → read real parabolas.

This is the pathway's first sustained work connecting **algebra and graphs**, so it deliberately builds the bridge between an equation and its picture — the same bridge the Intermediate Straight Lines unit built for linear graphs, now extended from straight lines to curves.

## Stepping Stones
Generate the terms of a quadratic sequence from a rule or a pattern · recognise a quadratic sequence by its **constant second difference** · use the second difference to continue a quadratic sequence and connect it to the coefficient of n² · expand a product of two simple linear expressions using an **area model** · factorise a simple quadratic x² + bx + c *(stretch)* · plot **y = x²** from a table of values and describe its **line of symmetry** · describe how the coefficient **a** stretches and (when a < 0) reflects the curve in **y = ax²** · describe how **p** translates the curve in **y = (x − p)²**, and locate its **turning point** and line of symmetry · compare the transformations (reflection, stretch, translation) of the basic parabola · interpret a real quadratic graph in context, reading and explaining its turning point as a **maximum or minimum**.

## Cornerstone emphasis across the unit
For this Algebra-and-graphs unit the natural spine is **Movement, Reflection and Connection**, with **Creativity** anchoring the applied finale. **Movement** is central — generating pattern terms, and dragging the a- and p-sliders to watch the parabola stretch, reflect and slide, is where the transformations become convincing rather than memorised. **Reflection** carries the unit's deep ideas: the **invariant second difference**, the line of symmetry, the turning point, and the side-by-side comparison of transformations. **Connection** binds the three faces — sequence ↔ expression ↔ curve — so the learner experiences them as one object. **Creativity** anchors Lesson 7, where learners interpret and pose their own real quadratic situations. **Rest** anchors the mid-unit checkpoint and the final consolidation.

### Nutrition — predominantly Mode B (intellectual)
Per GreenPrint D6, Mode A (contextual) is reserved for mathematics that genuinely lives in food, hydration, body, health, sustainability or budgeting. Most of this unit does not, so it must **not** let "real-world application" (a thrown ball, a bridge) masquerade as the Nutrition Cornerstone. The honest mode across QD-01–QD-06 is **Mode B — intellectual**, naming what the *learning itself* feeds:

- the nourishing discovery of an **invariant** — a second difference that stays fixed while the terms themselves grow without bound; the instinct to look for *what stays constant amid change* is one of the most transferable habits in mathematics;
- the intellectual satisfaction of seeing **one object with three faces** — that a number pattern, a symbol and a curve are the same quadratic — which builds the confidence that mathematics is connected, not a list of separate topics;
- **symmetry** as a source of certainty and economy (knowing one half of a parabola tells you the other).

**Lesson 7 may use Mode A honestly** where a context genuinely lives in those domains — for example maximising an enclosed growing-area for a fixed length of fencing (food-growing / sustainability), or a simple maximum-revenue decision (budgeting). Where the context is a thrown ball, a bridge or a fountain, it is used honestly as a **problem setting** and motivation, but the Nutrition panel stays **Mode B** and never presents the context *as* the Cornerstone.

## Reconnection Routes used
Substituting a value into an expression (evaluating x² and simple expressions) · squaring, including squaring a negative to a positive (from Pythagoras / Accuracy, Powers and Scale) · term-to-term and position-to-term sequences (KS3) · multiplying two brackets / the distributive law (for expanding) · plotting and reading coordinates on axes, including negative coordinates (KS3) · scale factor and translation as transformations (KS3 / Ratio). Several **algebra-facing routes are new to the Foundations register** (sequences, substitution, multiplying brackets, coordinate plotting) and will need a Stage 2 option bank before build, per the coverage-map convention for unbuilt routes.

## Resource reuse — audit before building
- **Parabola Lab (QD-04–QD-07):** the graphical spine. **Extend the Intermediate Straight Lines coordinate-graph engine** (the Dynamic Graph Lab) from straight lines to curves — plot y = x² from a table, then y = ax² and y = (x − p)² with live a/p controls, a turning-point marker and an axis-of-symmetry line. The axes, gridding, point-plotting and linked-representation machinery already exist; this unit adds curve plotting and the two transformation controls. Build the curve extension once, then preset it per lesson (Table Plotter, a-slider, p-slider, Parabola in the World).
- **Difference Machine (QD-01–QD-02):** a lightweight sequence table with first- and second-difference rows that fill in live as terms are entered or generated. New, but small; no coordinate engine needed.
- **Area-model expander (QD-03):** a rectangle-area / algebra-tiles model for expanding a product of two linear expressions, run in reverse for factorising. New — audit any existing area or grid interactive (e.g. from the 2D & 3D Shapes unit) for a reusable rectangle/area component before writing from scratch.
