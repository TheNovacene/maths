**Pathway:** Intermediate (internal stage code Y10) · **Term:** Autumn, Half-Term 1 — second unit
**Shape:** 8 lessons · 45 minutes each · fills the remaining weeks of HT1 after the 10-lesson Straight Lines unit (Intermediate runs 3 lessons/week)
**Qualification overlay:** Pearson Edexcel International GCSE (4MA1) — right-angled trigonometry; NC KS4 geometry (trigonometry). Sine/Cosine Rules and non-right triangles are explicitly **out of scope** for this introductory unit.
**Strand:** Geometry (follows the Foundations Pythagorean Theorem and 2D & 3D Shapes units)
**Status (v0.1):** blueprint framed; lessons built and reviewed one at a time.

## Unit Driving Question
In a right-angled triangle, once you fix an angle, the sides always hold the same ratios — so how can those ratios let us find a length, or an angle, that we cannot measure directly?

## The mathematical story
Trigonometry begins with a single, surprising fact: in a right-angled triangle, **once you fix one of the acute angles, the ratios between the sides are fixed too** — no matter how large or small you draw the triangle. Sine, cosine and tangent are simply names for those three constant ratios. This unit builds from that discovery rather than from a mnemonic: the learner first *notices* the constant ratio in similar right triangles, then learns to **name the sides relative to a chosen angle** (opposite, adjacent, hypotenuse) — the step where most early trigonometry stumbles. From there the ratios arrive one at a time — tangent first (no hypotenuse to manage), then sine and cosine, organised as **SOH CAH TOA**. The learner then puts the ratios to work: **finding an unknown side** from one side and one angle (first with the unknown on top, then rearranging when it is underneath or is the hypotenuse), and finally **finding an unknown angle** from two sides using the inverse ratios. It closes by choosing methods freely across mixed, real-world right-triangle problems — ramps, ladders, roof pitches and angles of elevation.

**Arc:** discover the constant ratio → name the sides → meet the ratios (tan, then sin & cos) → find a side → find an angle → apply & consolidate.

This unit builds directly on the **Pythagorean Theorem** work (right-angled triangles, the hypotenuse, "which side is which") and on **ratio / proportional reasoning**. It introduces deliberate, supported **calculator use in degrees** — a first for the pathway — treated as a skill to teach, not assume.

## Stepping Stones
Recognise that same-angle right triangles share fixed side ratios · name opposite, adjacent and hypotenuse relative to a given angle · define and use the tangent ratio (tan θ = opp ÷ adj) · define and use the sine and cosine ratios (SOH CAH TOA) · choose the correct ratio for a given problem · find an unknown side, including by rearranging · find an unknown angle using the inverse ratios (sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, tan⁻¹) · solve multi-step and real-world right-angled-triangle problems, checking answers for sense.

## Cornerstone emphasis across the unit
For the Geometry strand the GreenPrint's strongest Cornerstones are **Movement, Creativity and Rest**; in this unit **Connection** and **Reflection** also carry real weight. **Movement** is central — dragging the angle and watching the three ratios respond is where trigonometry becomes convincing. **Connection** binds an angle to its fixed ratio, and a side to its name. **Reflection** carries method-choice and checking (which ratio? is this answer sensible?). **Creativity** anchors the applied problem studio (Lesson 8), where learners pose and solve their own right-triangle situations. **Rest** anchors the mid-unit checkpoint and the final consolidation.

### Nutrition — Mode B (intellectual) throughout
Per GreenPrint D6, Mode A (contextual) is reserved for mathematics that genuinely lives in food, hydration, body, health, sustainability or budgeting. Trigonometry does not, so this unit does **not** force a nutritional context, and it must **not** let "real-world application" masquerade as the Nutrition Cornerstone. The honest mode here is **Mode B — intellectual**, and each lesson's Nutrition panel names what the *learning itself* feeds:

- the nourishing discovery of an **invariant** — a ratio that stays fixed while the size of the triangle changes freely; the instinct to look for *what stays constant amid change* is one of the most transferable habits in mathematics;
- the intellectual confidence and agency of **reaching what cannot be measured** — a height, a distance, an angle you can never physically get to — through reasoning alone;
- **precision** and disciplined tool-choice (which ratio, and why).

Every Nutrition panel therefore names Mode B, identifies what is nourished, offers a low-pressure way to notice what feels stronger, and asks the learner to carry one idea forward — never a generic wellbeing reflection and never a contrived context.

Real-world settings — ramps, ladders, roof pitches, angles of elevation — are used honestly as genuine **problem contexts** (especially in Lesson 8) and as motivation, but they are never presented *as* the Nutrition Cornerstone.

## Reconnection Routes used
Right-angled-triangle vocabulary and the hypotenuse (from the Pythagorean Theorem unit) · ratio and proportional reasoning · fractions as division (a ÷ b) · similarity (same shape, different size) · rearranging a simple multiplicative equation · supported calculator use in degrees. Assigned adaptively where a prerequisite check indicates need; the Pythagoras unit is the principal prior anchor.

## Resource reuse — one environment, many lessons
Like the Straight Lines unit (one shared graph environment) and unlike the bespoke-per-lesson 2D & 3D Shapes unit, this unit is built on **one shared Right-Triangle Trigonometry environment**: an interactive right-angled triangle with a **draggable acute angle**, live labelling of opposite / adjacent / hypotenuse *relative to the marked angle*, editable side lengths, and readouts of the three ratios. Each lesson is a **preset + framing** of that environment (Similar Triangles Explorer, Side Labeller, Tangent Explorer, Ratio Trio, Side Finder, Angle Finder, Problem Studio). This is a **new interactive spine** — the coordinate-graph engine from Straight Lines does not serve triangle geometry — so it is built once, then reskinned per lesson.

**Audit before building:** the Foundations **Pythagorean Theorem** lessons already render and manipulate right-angled triangles (the "hidden squares" / right-triangle interactives). Review those for a reusable triangle-drawing component before writing a new one from scratch, so the trig environment inherits a consistent triangle look and any existing drag/label machinery.

## Build approach
Frame one lesson → present a 3–5 option interactive bank (Stage 2) → build the full ecosystem (interactive, Practice Companion, Scratchpad, Learner + Supporting Adult guidance) → QA against the shipping gates → review. One lesson at a time; never pre-build ahead of review. British English, "learner" not "student", the six NEO Cornerstones in locked order, no year labels in learner-facing text, timings as invitations. Degrees-mode calculator use is introduced gently and named explicitly in the guidance.
