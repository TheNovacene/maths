## Unit Driving Question
With just a straightedge and a pair of compasses, how can we build shapes that are *exactly* right — and find *every* point that satisfies a rule?

## The mathematical story
Most drawing is approximate; **construction** is exact. This unit is about the surprising power of just two tools — a straightedge (an unmarked ruler, for straight lines) and a pair of **compasses** — to produce geometry you can *trust*, and about the single idea that makes them work.

That idea is **equal distance**. A circle is not really "a round shape"; it is **the set of all points the same distance from a centre**. The compass is a machine for "same distance", and once a learner sees that, every construction in the unit falls out of it. Copy a length by opening the compass to it. Build a triangle from three sides (SSS) as the meeting point of two circles. Find the **perpendicular bisector** — and see that it is not just a construction but a **locus**: the set of all points *equidistant from two fixed points*. Find the **angle bisector**, and see that it too is a locus: the set of all points *equidistant from two lines*. So the constructions and the loci are the same idea wearing two hats — *distance conditions drawn exactly*.

From there the unit widens. A **locus** is the path or region of every point obeying a condition: a fixed distance from a point is a **circle**; a fixed distance from a line is a **pair of parallel lines**; equidistant from two points is a **perpendicular bisector**; equidistant from two lines is an **angle bisector**. **Bearings** add *direction* to *distance* — a three-figure angle measured clockwise from north — so a position becomes "so far, in that direction", and a scale turns the drawing back into real ground. The unit closes with **compound constructions in context**: real "where should it go?" problems whose answer is the point or region where several loci meet — siting something on a scaled plan so that it satisfies two or three conditions at once.

**Arc:** the compass *is* equal distance → construct triangles and quadrilaterals from that → the perpendicular bisector as the *equidistant-from-two-points* locus → the angle bisector as the *equidistant-from-two-lines* locus → loci and regions in general → bearings (direction + distance + scale) → compound constructions that place a point where conditions meet.

This is the pathway's first sustained work in **exact geometry**, so it deliberately builds the habit that a construction must be *justified* by the equal-distance rule that produced it — the arcs are the argument, not decoration — the same reasoning-first stance the algebra units took towards structure.

## Stepping Stones
Understand a circle as the set of points a fixed distance from a centre, and use compasses to copy a length · construct a triangle given three sides (SSS), and given two sides and the included angle / two angles and a side using a protractor · construct simple quadrilaterals from given measurements · construct the **perpendicular bisector** of a segment, and describe it as the locus of points **equidistant from two points** · construct the **angle bisector**, and describe it as the locus of points **equidistant from two lines** · construct the perpendicular to a line **from** a point and **at** a point on the line · draw and interpret a **locus** for a given condition (fixed distance from a point, from a line, equidistant from two points or two lines) and shade the **region** satisfying an inequality condition · measure and draw **three-figure bearings**, and combine a bearing with a distance using a **scale** · solve a **compound construction** problem in context by finding where two or more loci meet on a scaled plan.

## Cornerstone emphasis across the unit
The natural spine is **Movement, Reflection and Connection**, with **Creativity** anchoring the applied finale. **Movement** is central and literal — swinging the compass, drawing along the straightedge, dragging a base point to watch a whole construction and its arcs update, and measuring bearings — this is where "construction" stops being a recipe and becomes an action the learner owns. **Reflection** carries the unit's deep idea: *why* a construction works — that the perpendicular bisector and angle bisector are **loci defined by equal distance**, and that the arcs are the justification. **Connection** binds constructions to loci to bearings: the same equal-distance idea generates the circle, both bisectors, and the compound regions. **Creativity** anchors Lesson 7, where learners pose and solve their own "where should it go?" problems. **Rest** anchors the mid-unit checkpoint and the final consolidation.

### Nutrition — predominantly Mode B (intellectual), with an honest Mode A finale
Per GreenPrint D6, Mode A (contextual) is reserved for mathematics that genuinely lives in food, hydration, body, health, sustainability or budgeting. Most of this unit does not, so it must **not** let "real-world" plans masquerade as the Nutrition Cornerstone. Across GC-01–GC-06 the honest mode is **Mode B — intellectual**, naming what the *learning* feeds: the deep satisfaction of **certainty from constraint** (that two humble tools, used honestly, produce results you can *prove*, not merely eyeball); the elegance of one idea — equal distance — generating a whole family of constructions; and the power of a **locus** as a way of thinking about *all* the solutions at once rather than one at a time.

**Lesson 7 may use Mode A honestly** where a compound-construction context genuinely lives in those domains — for example siting a **water trough or a new planting bed** equidistant from two paths and within reach of a tap (food-growing / sustainability / hydration), or placing a **well** to serve two dwellings fairly. Where the context is a treasure map or a phone-mast coverage problem, it is used honestly as a **problem setting** and motivation, but the Nutrition panel stays **Mode B**.

## Reconnection Routes used
Measuring a length accurately with a ruler (KS3) · measuring and drawing angles with a protractor (from the Introduction to Trigonometry unit) · properties of triangles and quadrilaterals — side and angle facts (from the 2D & 3D Shapes unit) · the idea of a circle and its radius (new to the register, introduced in GC-01) · parallel and perpendicular lines (KS3 / Straight Lines) · scale and ratio, and converting between a scaled drawing and real distances (from Ratio, for bearings). Several **construction-facing routes are new to the Foundations register** (the compass as an equal-distance tool, locus language, three-figure bearings) and will need a Stage 2 option bank before build, per the coverage-map convention for unbuilt routes.

## Resource reuse — audit before building
- **Dynamic Geometry Studio (GC-01–GC-07):** the unit's spine, and a **new reusable environment** — audited against existing interactives (the Dynamic Graph Lab / Parabola Lab is coordinate-and-curve based; the triangle engines from Trigonometry draw fixed labelled triangles; neither supports free construction), so a purpose-built dynamic-geometry engine is warranted. **Agreed scope (curriculum lead, this session):** a *fuller Sketchpad-style* studio with **authentic instruments that leave visible arcs**. Core capabilities: draggable points on a live **dependency graph** (drag a base point and the whole construction, including arcs, updates); a **compass** set to a radius and swung, and a **straightedge/segment** drawn along, both leaving their marks; **construct operations** (intersection, midpoint, perpendicular bisector, angle bisector, perpendicular/parallel line, locus/trace); **measurements** (length, angle, and a calculator read-out); **transformations** (reflect, rotate, translate, enlarge); a **protractor + north/bearings** mode with a scale; **animation** of a driver point; and **custom (saved) tools** so a completed construction can be reused. Fully inline, no third-party or CDN calls; honours *Reduce motion*; keyboard-reachable; within the visual-containment gate. **Build once as a standalone engine, sign off the architecture, then preset per lesson** (compass-and-straightedge → protractor triangles → bisectors → locus lab → bearings → scaled-plan compound). This mirrors how the Dynamic Graph Lab was built once and preset across Straight Lines and Quadratics.
- **Protractor / angle tools:** reuse and generalise the angle-measurement and drawing components from the Introduction to Trigonometry unit inside the Studio rather than writing new ones where possible.
- **Scratchpad:** the constructions Scratchpad background should offer a plain and a light-grid page and be genuinely optional; the Studio, not the Scratchpad, is where exact construction happens.

## Build approach
Because the Studio is a large, shared component, it is built and signed off **as a standalone reusable engine first** (architecture spec → build → QA of the engine itself), before GC-01. Thereafter, frame one lesson → present a 3–5 option interactive bank (Stage 2) drawn from Studio presets → build the full ecosystem (interactive, Practice Companion, Scratchpad, Learner + Supporting Adult guidance) → QA against the shipping gates **including QA gate 6a (equivalent-form answer acceptance)** → review. One lesson at a time; never pre-build ahead of review. British English, "learner" not "student", the six NEO Cornerstones in locked order, no chronological year labels in learner-facing text, timings as invitations, precise mathematical vocabulary (**perpendicular bisector, angle bisector, locus/loci, equidistant, bearing, construct** — glossed once on first use), honest Nutrition modes, and — for a constructions unit especially — the standard that a construction is judged by its **arcs and method**, not only its final picture. After each accepted lesson, offer to update the live Foundations Coverage Map (Stepping Stones, Reconnection Routes, status, evidence, header audit).
